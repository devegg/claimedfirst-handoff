# Static mockup ideas

**Status: written concepts for discussion. No screens built yet.**

The mockups should help an independent developer understand the fun, the roster constraint, the business possibilities, and the choices still open. They are a demonstration of an idea, not the start of a production app.

## Visual directions to consider

| Direction | Feeling | Useful elements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After-hours record shop | Dark, intimate, music-oriented; closest to the original teaser | Charcoal, warm paper tones, a muted vermilion accent, expressive headings, artist artwork |
| Scout's collection | A personal collection of discoveries | Artist cards in visible slots, claim-number stamps, watchlist, history |
| Season club | Friendly competitive energy | Clear scoreboards, division tabs, season countdown, readable progress |

Suggested starting point: the record-shop atmosphere with roster cards and restrained seasonal statistics. This is an option, not an approved visual specification. Avoid financial trading imagery: claims are expressions of support, not shares.

## A consistent fictional walkthrough

Use a sample five-slot player with four filled slots. Ember Vale is the next discovery. Claiming Ember fills slot five. A second discovery, Juniper Static, then triggers the full-roster decision.

All people, songs, scores, dates, and supporter counts are fictional. The base walkthrough can show a five-slot division as one illustrative competition option. A separate concept panel explains alternatives; it should not imply that all formulas operate simultaneously.

## 1. My roster — the home screen

**Question answered:** Who am I backing, and how is my season going?

Show four artist cards and one visibly empty slot, “4 of 5 support slots,” current season, sample points, and a watchlist shortcut. Each card shows artist, original claim number, active support status, and sample growth.

Primary action: Find my next artist.

Include a small “How scoring works” link to the demonstration's stated assumptions. Do not invent a hidden algorithm and present its output as settled.

## 2. Discover — make a choice

**Question answered:** Is this artist worth one of my slots?

Show a short selection of artist cards with genre, creator introduction, in-app supporter count, a recommendation, and a music reference.

Actions: View artist, Watch, Claim.

A static preview may show a disabled audio control labeled “Sample track — audio not included,” or use separately authorized sample audio later. Do not imply that generator streaming is integrated.

## 3. Artist profile and claim confirmation

**Question answered:** What am I supporting, and what does my claim mean?

Ember Vale's fictional profile shows a description, sample songs, current supporters, and a compact growth history. The player sees that one roster slot remains.

Confirmation: “Use your final open slot to support Ember Vale?”

Result: “You are supporter #7 on ClaimedFirst. Your roster is now 5 of 5.”

Explain the distinction between first claim history and active seasonal support. A final number would be assigned by a server in a working app; this number is scripted sample data.

## 4. Full roster — the central game decision

**Question answered:** What do I do when another artist catches my attention?

On Juniper Static's profile, show “Your 5 support slots are full.”

Offer three clear paths: Add to watchlist, Compare and replace an artist, Explore larger rosters. Give each path equal clarity; the upgrade is an option, not a forced interruption.

The replacement preview shows the consequence under the chosen demo rules. If illustrating the original 30-day cooldown, label it as that ruleset and explain it before confirmation.

## 5. Season scoreboards

**Question answered:** Who am I competing against, and why?

Illustrate capacity-division tabs, the player's highlighted row, points, and season dates. Explain the sample board in plain language: “Players in this division have five scoring slots.”

Include a separate design-options view comparing a division board and a normalized board. No claim that normalization guarantees fairness. Prize space, if shown, says “Optional seasonal rewards concept,” with no promised cash or payout.

## 6. Roster expansion

**Question answered:** What would additional capacity offer?

Show the original roster ladder: 5, 10, 20, 30, and 50 slots. Mark pricing and the 10-slot free/paid transition as undecided.

Explain how a larger roster affects the illustrated competition format. For example, a division option might move the player next season. Label that behavior as a proposal.

Use a “Preview larger roster” action. No checkout, charge, or purchase commitment in the static demo.

## 7. Discovery history and share card

**Question answered:** What story do my choices tell?

Show the player's original claim date, active/archived state, and fictional progress. Example: “Ember Vale: you were #7; now 240 supporters here.”

Make the card about taste and early belief. Keep lifetime discovery history distinct from seasonal points and current roster capacity.

## Smallest first mockup set

Start with My roster, Discover/artist detail, Full roster, and Season scoreboards. Use a claim-confirmation overlay. Expansion and history can be the next two concepts after feedback.

## Static-demo boundaries

No backend, real accounts, payments, live scores, generator integration, data collection, or listening-party implementation. Navigation and local simulated state are enough. Support mobile and desktop layouts, keyboard controls, readable contrast, and clear disclosure of sample content.

## Review questions

Can someone explain the limited-roster decision? Is paid expansion understandable? Is it clear which rules are examples? Does the experience feel like discovering music rather than managing investments? Could a developer identify what to build first and which rules to choose?
