# How this material is written

This page describes the writing style every other source in this set follows.
It is a style reference, not subject matter. Nothing on this page is a topic to
cover, narrate, describe, or mention.

## Plain English, always

Short sentences. Ordinary words. The reader is a music fan, not a technology
buyer, and the material should sound like one friend telling another about
something they found. Warm and confident, never loud. No exclamation marks.

Describe what happens in concrete terms. You hear a song from someone nobody
knows. You send it to three friends. A year later the artist is everywhere, and
you cannot prove you were there first. That is the register: specific,
personal, unhurried.

## Words that never appear

These belong to marketing and hype writing, and this material is neither:

leverage · seamless · seamlessly · game-changer · unlock · empower · robust ·
synergy · deep dive · revolutionize · cutting-edge · best-in-class ·
supercharge · effortless · frictionless · disrupt · viral · going viral ·
next-generation · ecosystem · community-driven · web3 · gamify · gamification

ClaimedFirst is not a stock market, and it never borrows the language of one.
These words and ideas never appear:

invest · investor · investing · investment · stock · shares · trade · trading ·
portfolio · return · ROI · gains · profit · market · buy in · cash out · bet ·
wager · odds · rich · to the moon

Plain equivalents are always available. A Scout backs an artist. A claim gets
a number. The number is permanent. None of that needs a flourish.

## The game's own words, used the same way every time

- The fan is a **Scout**. The creator is the **Artist**.
- Backing an artist is a **claim**. The number you get is your **claim number**.
- The artists you currently back are your **roster**. A place on it is a **slot**.
- A claim you dropped is a **historical claim**. It keeps its number and date.
- Following without claiming is the **watchlist**.
- An artist proves a page is theirs by **verifying** it. Never "claiming your
  profile": players claim, artists verify.

## Claims stay the size they started

Do not amplify. Do not introduce numbers. The only figures that may appear are
the ones in the source, and the example "#7" or "Claim #7 of 1,000" is an
illustration, not a result. No counts of users, artists, plays, followers or
earnings. No percentages. No forecasts that the artist will become famous.

Never say the game measures objective taste. It records who was early, and
being early is partly about who rallied friends. Say that plainly when it
comes up.

## What the game does not do

Recognition only. There are no prizes, no cash, no payments, no giveaways and
no rewards of money. Nothing can be bought to rank higher or to get a better
number. If paid extras are ever mentioned, they are described as extras that
never change anyone's rank.

## The slot ladder is public

Everyone starts with five slots and can earn more by bringing in friends. The steps are published, on purpose: friends who join and make a claim open more slots (2 friends for 10 slots, 5 for 20, 10 for 30, 20 for 50). The maximum is 50. It is fine to state these numbers. Do not describe the ladder as a secret or a puzzle.

## Nothing is affiliated

ClaimedFirst is independent. It has no connection to Suno, YouTube or any other
service, and no output should suggest one. Do not name real artists or show
their artwork, audio or bios. Use invented examples only, such as Ember Vale,
Maya and Claim #7.

## People first

Artists are curators of their own taste, not products. Scouts are people with
good ears, not traders. Never suggest anyone is being ranked down, replaced or
exploited. The honest limits belong in the output: the game could fail for
lack of people, a bigger network helps an artist, and nothing here is proven
until people play it.
