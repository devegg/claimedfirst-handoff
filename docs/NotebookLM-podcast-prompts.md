## Angle A: From the artist's chair

### Short (under 500 characters)

> Tell this from the artist's side. One host makes music with AI tools and is wary; the other is curious. Cover what being claimed means to a small artist, why verifying with a bio code respects stage names, what Top Songs do, how fans can stay anonymous, and why an artist is really a curator of their own fans. Warm, funny, grounded. No money or trading language. Don't give exact slot numbers; say more slots unlock by bringing friends. Say plainly that demand is unproven.

### Long

> Make this a conversation from the artist's side, about 12 to 15 minutes. Host 1 is an independent musician who makes songs with AI tools and has been burned by platforms that ignore small creators; Host 2 is a curious music fan. Walk through a day in the life of an artist on ClaimedFirst: finding out strangers have claimed their page, verifying with a one-time code in their bio without giving up a stage name, choosing Top Songs, seeing who their earliest supporters are (some named, some anonymous by choice), and adding a donation link the app never touches. Explore the idea that an artist is a kind of curator, and that the fans they attract are a curated taste. Include the worries honestly: impersonation, being added without asking, popularity beating talent, and whether anyone will show up. Tone: warm, funny, grounded, not salesy. Do not use trading, investing or stock-market language. Do not state the exact referral thresholds; say only that more slots unlock by bringing friends. There are no prizes. Say clearly that nobody has proved people want this yet. End with one question each host would ask a first artist before they verify.

## Angle B: The skeptics' hour

### Short (under 500 characters)

> Make this a friendly debate. One host is a skeptic, the other defends the idea. Take the three hardest objections in the sources (empty room at launch, fake accounts, popularity over taste) and test each honestly, including where the answer is weak. No hype, no money or trading language, no exact slot numbers. Say demand is unproven. End on what would have to be true for this to work and the cheapest way to find out.

### Long

> Make this a lively but respectful debate of about 12 to 15 minutes. Host 1 is a skeptic who has watched many social apps fail; Host 2 believes in the idea but must earn every point. Take the hardest objections from the sources one at a time: the empty-room problem at launch, fake accounts and farming, popularity beating taste, artists being added without consent, and whether permanent numbers will actually make people share. For each, state the strongest version of the objection, then the answer in the sources, then whether the skeptic is satisfied. Admit where answers are thin. No hype. Do not use trading, investing or stock-market language. Do not state exact referral thresholds; say more slots unlock by bringing friends. There are no prizes. State clearly that demand is unproven. Finish with the one cheap test that would show whether people care, and what result would mean stop.

## Angle C: The first 24 hours (storytelling)

### Short (under 500 characters)

> Tell it as a story, not an explainer: one fictional launch day following three people: a small artist, the first scout to claim her, and a friend who gets the shared card. Keep it vivid and funny, with real feelings at each step: nerves, a permanent number, a little pride, a little FOMO. Weave the rules in only as they happen. No money or trading language, no exact slot numbers, no prizes. Remind listeners it is a fictional scenario for an idea not yet built.
