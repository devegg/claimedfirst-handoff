# ClaimedFirst: the full story

*A plain-English overview for turning into a podcast, a slide deck and infographics. Every person, artist and number in the examples is made up. ClaimedFirst is built and is about to be tried by a small group of testers before any public launch. Nobody has yet shown that people want it. It is independent: it has no connection to Suno, YouTube or any other company mentioned, and none is implied.*

---

## 1. The idea in one sentence

ClaimedFirst is a game where you prove you found an AI music artist before everyone else did, and nobody can ever take that proof away.

## 2. The feeling behind it

Almost everyone has felt this: you hear a song from someone nobody knows, you send it to your friends, and a year later that artist is everywhere. You want credit for your ear, but you can't prove it. "I knew them before they were famous" is just a claim with nothing behind it.

ClaimedFirst turns that feeling into something you can show. When you back an artist, the app gives you a number: the order in which you arrived. Be the seventh person to back an artist and you are **Claim #7**, forever. If that artist grows to 4,000 fans, "#7 out of 4,000" is a trophy that can't be bought, grinded for or faked.

It is a game about taste and reputation. It is deliberately **not** a stock market. You are not buying shares, trading, or investing money. You are putting your name next to an artist you believe in.

## 3. Why AI music, and why now

AI tools such as Suno let almost anyone make a full song. The result is a flood of new artists and almost no way to discover the good ones early. The big platforms are built for listening, not for discovering people before they take off. That gap is where ClaimedFirst lives.

AI music creators are the starting point. If it works there, the same idea could later apply to podcasts, indie games, newsletters or YouTubers.

## 4. How the game works

### Scouts, artists and claims

- The fan is called a **Scout**.
- The creator is the **Artist**.
- Backing an artist is a **Claim**.
- The artists you currently back are your **Roster**.

### Your claim number is permanent

The server hands out numbers in order, one at a time, so two people can never get the same number. Your number never changes.

### Dropping an artist

You can drop an artist whenever you like, but you keep the proof. A dropped claim stays on your profile forever as a **Historical claim**, with its original number and date. The slot it used becomes free.

If you want to come back to that artist, you have to wait **30 days**. When you return you get a brand-new, later number, and your old Historical claim stays too. Walking away costs you something, and that's on purpose.

### Limited slots, and how you get more

Everyone starts with **5 slots**, so every pick is a real decision. You earn more slots by bringing in friends who join and make a claim:

| Friends who joined and made a claim | Slots you have |
|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 10 |
| 5 | 20 |
| 10 | 30 |
| 20 | 50 (the maximum) |

The steps are public, and your invite page shows each friend as joined, pending, or counted. A friend only counts once their account is a few days old and they have made at least one claim. That stops people from making fake accounts to farm slots.

### The watchlist

You can follow up to 100 artists without claiming them (a larger watchlist could be a paid perk later). Watching earns nothing and gives you no number. You can watch their claim counts climb and decide when to jump in.

### You decide who sees you

When you claim an artist, you choose who sees your name: **show my name** (the artist, the Founders board and your profile all show @yourname), **artist only** (the artist knows you're a fan, but everyone else sees only "Anonymous scout"), or **anonymous** (nobody sees your name, not even the artist). Your number and points are the same either way. Watching is simpler: either the artist can see your name, or nobody can. The public never sees who is watching. You can change any of these later, and there are buttons to change them all at once.

### Scoring

Backing an artist early counts for more:

| Your claim number | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 1 to 10 | 5x |
| 11 to 50 | 3x |
| 51 to 200 | 2x |
| 201 and up | 1x |

Your points come from how many new, genuine fans an artist gains after you backed them, multiplied by your multiplier. Only active claims earn points. Seasons run monthly, so new players always get a fresh start. Your claim numbers and history are permanent and never reset.

Everything is **recognition only**. There are no prizes and no cash. The reward is the number and the bragging rights.

### The boards

- **Artist Founders board:** on each artist's page, the 100 earliest people ever to back them. Dropped claims are labeled "Historical".
- **Scout's Historical 100:** on each Scout's profile, their 100 earliest claim numbers.
- **Season leaderboard:** the monthly ranking by points.

A claim only shows up on the Founders and Historical boards after it has been held for **14 days**. Without that rule, someone could claim lots of artists and drop them immediately just to collect trophies.

## 5. The shareable moment

The heart of the app is a card that writes itself:

> "Maya was #7 on Ember Vale. Be #8."

After you claim an artist, the app makes a card in story size or square size that you can post anywhere. Later, when that artist reaches 10, 100 or 1,000 fans, you get a new card: "I was #7 of 1,000."

A friend who taps the link lands on the artist's page, sees "Be #8", and can claim in a couple of taps. They don't have to install anything to look around.

## 6. Why it can spread

Here is the clever part. Everyone who backs an artist after you makes your claim more impressive. So **sharing an artist only ever helps you.** Telling friends about Ember Vale pushes your "#7 out of 4,000" higher. The scoring itself rewards spreading the word.

Ways it travels:

1. **Cards and links.** Every claim produces something worth posting, with a link that previews well on Discord, X, Reddit and in messages.
2. **Artists as promoters.** Verified artists get a "Claimed by 1,204 fans" badge and a button they can paste into their Suno bio, YouTube description or link-in-bio page. Every artist who does this advertises the app to their own fans.
3. **Small boards.** Few people brag about being #48,000 in the world. Many will brag about being the top Scout in their Discord server or friend group.
4. **Referral slots.** Bringing friends is the main way to earn more slots, so growth is built into the game.
5. **Original Scouts.** A permanent badge for the earliest Scouts is planned. The exact rule is still being decided.

## 7. The artist side

Anyone can add an artist, but it is clearly labeled **"Fan-created page, not verified by the artist"** until the real artist proves it's theirs.

### How an artist proves it's them

1. The artist taps "Verify" and gets a one-time code, like `cf-K7M4QX`.
2. They paste it into the bio or description of one of their own public pages: their Suno profile, YouTube channel, SoundCloud, TikTok or website.
3. ClaimedFirst reads that public page, looks for the code, and awards a verified badge.

This proves the artist controls that account. It does not reveal anyone's real name, which suits artists who prefer a stage identity. This check has already been tried for real on a Suno profile and a YouTube channel, from a home computer and from a cloud server, and it worked each time.

### What verified artists get

- A badge and an embeddable "Claimed by N" button.
- A private list of the scouts who claimed them (with claim numbers) and the scouts who are watching them. Each scout chooses whether the artist sees their name. Anonymous scouts still count in the totals, but an artist can never find out who they are.
- **Top Songs:** they choose up to 10 links they most want fans to claim.
- An optional donation link, which is just a link out to Ko-fi, PayPal or similar. ClaimedFirst never handles the money.
- The power to freeze new claims, edit their page or remove it entirely.

If someone is impersonating an artist, the real artist can verify and take over the page. Anyone can report a page, which pauses new claims until it's sorted out.

### What ClaimedFirst never does

It never copies artwork, audio or bios from other sites. It only links out, and shows plain generated placeholder records. Artists can choose a record style, but there are no image uploads for now: uploads need safety checks and cost money to run, so they may come later as a small paid customization.

## 8. How it could pay for itself, honestly

Early on, running costs are small, and a donation link for the founder can cover them. If it grows:

1. **Keep referral as the free road to every slot, forever.** Later, add an optional paid "skip ahead" that never takes away earned slots and never gives better scoring.
2. **Charge artists, not fans.** Verified artists could pay for extras like messaging their earliest supporters and deeper analytics.
3. **Cosmetics.** Profile frames and badges that show style but not skill.
4. **A voice in the app (a future perk).** Paying members could post suggestions in a suggestion box and vote on each other's suggestions, so the people who support the app help steer it. Suggestions would guide the plans, not bind them.

The hard rule: **you can never pay to rank higher or to get a better claim number.**

A larger company in AI music could one day want something like this: engaged fans, a network of creators, and a signal of which new artists people believe in. That's a hope, not a plan. What keeps the door open is clean ownership, no copied content, and an app that doesn't depend on any single platform.

## 9. The risks, plainly

1. **The empty-room problem.** A game about being early needs people and artists on day one. The plan is to recruit 20 to 50 creators with followings before launch.
2. **Fake accounts.** The 7-day account rule, the 14-day board rule, rate limits and bans are the defenses. They help, but no system is perfectly safe.
3. **Popularity, not taste.** Because growth is counted inside the app, artists with bigger networks do better. It measures who rallied people, not who is objectively best, and the app shouldn't claim otherwise.
4. **Impersonation.** Handled with labels, bio-code verification and reporting.
5. **Money and legal.** Before launch it needs a company, terms of service and a privacy policy. If prizes or paid features are ever added, they would need legal advice first.
6. **Dependence on other sites.** The game never relies on Suno's or YouTube's systems for its rules. The only outside touch is reading a public bio page to verify an artist, and that could be blocked someday, so there is a manual backup.

## 10. What exists today

1. **A working website**, live at claimedfirst.com, with sign-in, claiming, a roster, a discover page, boards, share cards and artist verification.
2. **One example artist page** on the live demo site, marked as a fan-created page until the artist verifies it. Page addresses use the artist's own Suno handle, the way many sites use @names.
3. **A tested proof** that artist verification works.
4. **A written design, a build plan and a test suite** covering the claim numbers, privacy choices and scoring.

Next: a trial with a small group, then a pause to talk about how it plays. Claims made during the trial will be wiped before any public launch. Still undecided: the Original Scout rule, and how the app pays for itself.

## 11. Numbers to remember

- 5 starting slots, with more earned by bringing friends.
- 30-day wait to re-add a dropped artist.
- 14 days before a claim appears on a board.
- 7 days before a friend or fan "counts".
- 3 independent scouts (or 1 verification) to put an artist page live.
- 100 spots on each board.
- Up to 10 Top Songs per verified artist.
- 5x, 3x, 2x, 1x early-claim multipliers.
- 100 artists on a watchlist.

## 12. Glossary

- **Claim:** backing an artist, which gives you a permanent number.
- **Claim number:** your place in line for that artist.
- **Historical claim:** a dropped claim that keeps its original number and date.
- **Roster:** the artists you back right now.
- **Slot:** one place on your roster.
- **Watchlist:** artists you follow without backing.
- **Anonymous scout:** someone who chose not to show their name to the artist or on the boards. They still count.
- **Verify:** an artist proving a page is theirs with a one-time code.
- **Founders board:** the 100 earliest backers of one artist.
- **Original Scout:** one of the first 100 accounts.
