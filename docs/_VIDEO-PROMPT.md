## [START PROMPT](no-such-file.md)

Make a short video that explains one idea clearly to a music fan: ClaimedFirst,
a game where you prove you found an artist before everyone else did.

**AUDIENCE AND TONE**

Speak to someone who loves finding small artists and sharing them with friends.
The voice is one friend telling another about something they found: warm,
calm, a little amused, never loud. Short sentences. Ordinary words. No
exclamation marks.

This is not an advertisement. Nothing is being sold and nobody is being asked to
join, sign up, download or donate.

**STRUCTURE**

Open with the feeling as it physically happens. You hear a song from someone
nobody has heard of. You send it to three friends. A year later that artist is
everywhere, and you have no way to show you were there first. Do not open with
a question, a statistic, or a claim about how big the music world is.

State the honest qualification early and plainly: this is an idea built and
about to be tried by a small group, nobody has shown that people want it yet,
and it could fail. It is the most important thing in the video and must
survive into the final narration.

Then work through, in this order, making each point once:
  - the number: when you back an artist you get a claim number, the order you
    arrived, and it is yours permanently
  - why the roster is small: everyone starts with five slots, so every pick is
    a choice, and friends who join and make a claim open more
  - what dropping an artist does: you keep the number and the date as a
    historical claim, and coming back later takes a wait and a new number
  - who sees your name: you choose, for each claim
  - what you can share: a card that says what number you were
  - why it spreads: every person who backs an artist after you makes your
    number mean more, so telling friends only helps you
  - the artist side: every page is marked as made by fans until the artist
    proves it is theirs with a short code in their own profile
  - what it is not: recognition only, no prizes, no money, nothing to buy

End on the human part: the number only keeps the proof. The ear that found the
artist first was yours, and the artist decides what happens to their own page.
That is the point of the whole video, not a closing courtesy.

**RULES THAT MATTER MORE THAN ANYTHING ELSE**

Never invent a number. No counts of users, artists, plays, followers or
earnings, no percentages, no forecasts that any artist will become famous. The
only figures allowed are ones in the sources, and "Claim #7" is an
illustration, not a result.

Never amplify a claim. Keep every claim the size it started. Never say the game
measures objective taste: it records who was early, and a bigger network helps.

Never use the language of money or markets. ClaimedFirst is not a stock market.
No investing, trading, shares, portfolios or returns.

Never name a real artist, a company, a platform, a music service or a person.
Never suggest any affiliation with one. Use only the invented examples from the
sources.

Never suggest anyone is ranked down, replaced or exploited. Artists are
curators of their own taste and Scouts are people with good ears.

**DESCRIBE, DO NOT POINT**

Explain what happens, not what the viewer is looking at. No narration like
"this card shows" or "watch the number appear." Say what occurs; let the
visuals carry themselves.

**LANGUAGE**

Follow the attached writing style document exactly, including its list of words
that never appear and its game vocabulary.

**WHAT TO DO WITH THE SOURCES**

The overview is the subject. The two style documents describe how the material
should sound and look; they are references, not subject matter, so never
quote, mention or depict anything from them, and never discuss writing style,
colors, fonts or layout in the video.

Make every point once. Do not repeat a point in different words to fill time.
If there is not enough material for the length, make a shorter video.

**LENGTH AND FORMAT**

Two to three minutes, landscape. Five to seven scenes, one idea each.

## Short version (for a small prompt box)

> A calm two-minute explainer for music fans about ClaimedFirst, a game where
> you prove you found an artist first. Open with hearing a song nobody knows and
> sending it to friends. Say early that it is an untested idea. Cover the
> permanent claim number, five slots, dropping keeps the number, you choose who
> sees your name, the share card, why sharing helps you, artists verify their
> own page, and recognition only with no prizes. End on the Scout's ear. Never
> invent numbers, never name real artists or companies, never state how many
> friends earn more slots, no money or stock-market language, no exclamation
> marks.

## How to judge a generation

In this order, because these are not equally important:

1. **Anything false** — an invented number, an amplified claim, a real artist
   or company named, a prize or payment implied.
2. **Anything off-message** — stock-market language, the honest qualification
   missing, a claim that the game measures taste, a pitch to sign up.
3. **Everything else** — mild repetition, a slightly strong word, an imperfect
   transition. Log it; do not regenerate for it.

## Likely weaknesses to watch for

These were real problems in the Ridgeline tests, so look for them first:

- stage directions such as "watch this card appear";
- the qualification dropped or softened;
- hype words and exclamation marks;
- invented examples that sound like findings ("thousands of Scouts").
